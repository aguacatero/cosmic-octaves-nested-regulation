# Adversarial Evidence Review

**Date:** 2026-09-18  
**Purpose:** Force the project to represent the strongest evidence against its preferred hypotheses as carefully as evidence that appears supportive.

## Principle

A durable argument is not one that collects confirming examples. It is one that survives the best competing explanations.

For every important claim, this project should maintain three things side by side:

1. strongest supportive evidence;
2. strongest critical/alternative evidence;
3. a predeclared test capable of discriminating between them.

---

# 1. VASCO / POSS-I transients

## Supportive side

### Bruehl & Villarroel 2025 — Scientific Reports

**Paper:** *Transients in the Palomar Observatory Sky Survey (POSS-I) may be associated with nuclear testing and reports of unidentified anomalous phenomena*  
**DOI:** `10.1038/s41598-025-21620-3`

Reported statistical associations between candidate transient counts and atmospheric nuclear-test timing, with additional analyses involving UAP-report timing.

### Doherty 2026 — independent preprint

**Paper:** *Independent Replication of Nuclear Test-Transient Correlations and Earth Shadow Deficit in POSS-I Photographic Plates*  
**arXiv:** `2604.00056`

Reports reproduction of the nuclear-window association and Earth-shadow deficit using the original dataset plus additional regression/permutation analysis.

**Status caution:** independent analysis is valuable, but this is a preprint and does not by itself resolve dataset-validity objections.

### Bruehl et al. 2026 — Scientific Reports

**Paper:** *Use of machine learning to enhance detection of transient astronomical phenomena in historical observatory images*  
**DOI:** `10.1038/s41598-026-66148-2`

Used an ML classifier trained on expert-reviewed image pairs to assign artifact/real probabilities. The authors report persistence of nuclear-window and Earth-shadow effects after controlling/filtering by ML-derived artifact probability.

## Critical side

### Watters et al. 2026 — Publications of the Astronomical Society of Australia

**Paper:** *Critical Evaluation of Studies Alleging Evidence for Technosignatures in the POSS1-E Photographic Plates*  
**DOI:** `10.1017/pasa.2026.10230`

Published online 23 July 2026 as a peer-reviewed accepted manuscript.

The authors challenge several load-bearing assumptions/results, including:

- inconsistencies in dataset definitions;
- unvalidated features that may include catalogue stars, scan artifacts, and plate defects;
- lack of demonstration that all analyzed features are optical transients;
- plate-position/systematic structure;
- linear-cluster feature validity;
- Earth-shadow result on a more vetted subset;
- nuclear-test correlation after normalization for Palomar observation days/schedule.

## Response from the VASCO side

### Villarroel et al. 2026 — preprint response

**Paper:** *A Response to paper Critical Evaluation of Studies Alleging Evidence for Technosignatures in the POSS1-E Photographic Plates by Watters et al. (2026)*  
**arXiv:** `2602.15171`

The authors argue that the critique conflates object-level validation with ensemble-level inference, uses a heavily reduced/heterogeneous subset, lacks power for some tests, and does not invalidate the main reported effects.

## Current project judgment

**Status: CONTESTED.**

The project should not resolve this disagreement by choosing the paper most consistent with the larger hypothesis.

### Required adjudication

A serious replication should predeclare and compare at least:

- dataset-version definitions;
- feature-validation criteria;
- plate-level and position-level systematics;
- actual observation opportunities/days;
- weather/seeing/environmental covariates where justified;
- artifact probability / image-level validation;
- Earth-shadow geometry calculation;
- temporal window definitions;
- multiple-comparison handling;
- sensitivity to alternative defensible inclusion rules;
- discovery vs confirmation data.

The ideal result is an analysis where both supportive and critical authors can point to their preferred model **before** the holdout is opened.

# 2. Patrick Jackson / coordinated sphere network

## Potentially supportive material

Jackson publicly describes a proposed distributed sphere architecture with differentiated roles, relays, sensing/response behavior, and coordinated operation.

## Strongest current objection

The project has not identified a peer-reviewed, frozen event catalog and quantitative model demonstrating that this network exists.

Narrative consistency across cases is not enough. Apparent geometries may result from:

- selection effects;
- shared environment;
- reporting templates;
- sensor geometry;
- clustering by chance;
- post-hoc pattern construction;
- heterogeneous case quality.

## Current project judgment

**Status: MODEL GENERATOR / UNTESTED.**

Jackson's value to this project is currently that his model may generate predictions that can fail.

### Required adjudication

Before examining confirmation data, define:

- expected network motifs;
- role classes and observable signatures;
- temporal ordering/relay predictions;
- persistence expectations;
- null independent-event model;
- survey/sensor geometry alternatives;
- exact out-of-sample success/failure thresholds.

# 3. Observer-channel modulation / ECM analogy

## Why the hypothesis is interesting

Electronic countermeasures suggest a concrete analogy: an adversarial system can act on the **information channel** rather than physically changing the underlying target. The observer hypothesis asks whether perception, memory, interpretation, or reporting could show structured distortion relative to independent channels.

## Strongest conventional alternatives

Any apparent observer effect must compete against well-established causes of discrepancy:

- signal-detection limits;
- inattentional blindness;
- expectation/priming;
- predictive perception;
- stress/arousal;
- memory reconstruction;
- source-monitoring error;
- suggestion/social conformity;
- rumor/media contagion;
- prior belief;
- ordinary fraud/deception;
- reporting-selection bias;
- sensor/instrument limitations.

## Current project judgment

**Status: UNTESTED.**

Witness discrepancy is not evidence of consciousness manipulation.

### Required adjudication

The first credible observer study should use independent/objective channels and matched conventional events. A modulation model should predict unseen observer errors better than ordinary cognitive/social models and should predefine whether the predicted error is directional (for example toward ambiguity/concealment) rather than merely larger.

# 4. Vallée control-system interpretation

## Why it matters

Vallée's work motivates the possibility that the phenomenon is not simply a set of objects but an adaptive control process influencing witnesses, beliefs, or society.

## Strongest objection

A pattern of `disturbance → unusual event` is not enough to establish a control system.

Regulation requires:

- system boundary;
- protected state/viability region;
- disturbance/error;
- corrective direction;
- feedback;
- recovery/adaptation;
- alternatives that fail to explain the same observations.

## Current project judgment

**Status: THEORETICAL/HISTORICAL PROVENANCE.**

Vallée's model generates research questions. It is not evidence that regulation exists.

# 5. Cosmic Octaves / scale recurrence

## Potentially supportive idea

A recurring scale relationship could indicate non-random organization worth formal study.

## Strongest current objection

The prior Rank-1 analysis in this repository was invalidated because of a mathematical defect. Scale lists and pair selection are vulnerable to researcher degrees of freedom and selection effects.

## Current project judgment

**Status: UNRESOLVED.**

### Required adjudication

Use corrected mathematics, frozen inclusion rules, explicit null ensembles, sensitivity analysis, and a holdout or independently assembled scale set.

# Standard for future evidence reviews

For every new bridge or high-level claim, add a section containing:

1. best evidence for;
2. best evidence against;
3. unresolved methodological disagreements;
4. a test with a result that would count against the preferred hypothesis;
5. a statement of what the result would **not** establish even if positive.

This file should become more uncomfortable, not less, as the project improves.
